Editable Car Motion & Kinematics Simulator

PHYS101iE — Physics I | Dr. Mohammed Al Bahri

1. Choose a motion scenario

All motion is along one straight x-axis. Positive velocity = right; negative velocity = left.

2. Design your own velocity graph

Edit the time and velocity at each point. The line between adjacent points represents constant acceleration. To obtain constant velocity, give two successive points the same velocity.

PointTime (s)Velocity (m/s)

Times must increase from 0 s. You can use negative velocities and add as many stages as needed (up to 12).

3. Run the simulation

Teaching idea: Start with the negative velocity preset. Change two velocities to −3 m/s to create a constant-velocity interval. Then change a later point to 0 m/s to make the car stop.

Displacement–time

x (m) against t (s) · slope = velocity

Velocity–time

v (m/s) against t (s) · signed area = displacement

Acceleration–time

a (m/s²) against t (s) · slope of v–t
The simulator computes displacement from the area under the velocity–time graph: Δx = ∫ v dt. Acceleration is the slope a = Δv/Δt. For each straight v–t segment, x = x₀ + v₀Δt + ½a(Δt)². At a corner in the v–t graph, instantaneous acceleration is not defined; the a–t graph shows the acceleration on either side.
